What if the entire history of the Universe happened in one year?

Take approximately 13.8 billion years of cosmic history and compress it into a single calendar. The Big Bang happens at midnight on January 1. The present arrives at the final moment of December 31.

THE SCALE

One year.

The Cosmic Calendar is a visualization of cosmic history in which the Universe's entire approximately 13.8-billion-year history is mapped onto one Gregorian year.

It is a scale model — not a new physical unit of time. The dates below are therefore approximate.

13.8 billion years → 365 days

On this scale, approximately 37.8 million years pass every calendar day.

THE YEAR

The Universe, month by month.

Select a month to jump through cosmic history. The timeline moves from the earliest observable Universe toward the moment in which you are reading this page.

January

The beginning
JAN 01 · 00:00:00

The Universe

The Universe begins its hot, dense expansion approximately 13.8 billion years ago. Modern cosmology can describe the Universe back to extremely early times, although the physics of the very beginning remains an open question.

JAN 01 · FIRST MOMENTS

Inflation

Cosmological models include an extraordinarily rapid early expansion known as inflation. Its physical origin remains an active area of research.

JAN 01 · MINUTES

Primordial nucleosynthesis

Within the first few minutes, conditions allow light atomic nuclei — primarily hydrogen and helium, with tiny amounts of other light nuclei — to form.

February

Cooling cosmos
FEBRUARY

A young, expanding Universe

The Universe continues expanding and cooling. Matter evolves from the extremely hot early state toward the structures that will eventually become stars and galaxies.

March

Atoms and darkness
JAN 01 · 00:00:01

Recombination

About 380,000 years after the Big Bang, electrons combine with atomic nuclei to form neutral atoms. Light can now travel much more freely. This ancient light is observed today as the cosmic microwave background.

COSMIC DARK AGES

No stars yet

After the cosmic microwave background was released, the Universe entered a period without stars. Hydrogen and helium filled an increasingly structured cosmos.

April

The first lights
~JAN 06

First stars

The first stars ignite after hundreds of millions of years of cosmic evolution. Their intense radiation transforms their surroundings. These stars also become factories for elements heavier than those produced in the early Universe.

EARLY COSMIC ERA

First galaxies

Stars gather under gravity into increasingly complex structures. Galaxies grow through star formation, gravitational interactions and mergers.

May

Cosmic architecture
MAY

Galaxies evolve

Galaxies grow and transform. Stars are born and die. Their deaths return newly forged elements to interstellar space.

June

Stars become chemical factories
JUNE

Heavy elements accumulate

Successive generations of stars enrich galaxies with elements heavier than hydrogen and helium. These materials eventually become part of future stars, planets and planetary systems.

July

A structured Universe
JULY

The cosmic web

Gravity organizes matter into galaxies, galaxy groups, clusters and enormous filamentary structures separated by cosmic voids.

August

The Milky Way era
AUGUST

Our galaxy takes shape

The Milky Way develops through the assembly and evolution of stellar populations and smaller systems over cosmic time.

September

Our cosmic neighbourhood
~SEP 01

The Sun

About 4.6 billion years ago, a cloud of gas and dust in the Milky Way collapses to form the Sun and the surrounding planetary system.

~SEP 01

Earth

Earth forms within the young Solar System approximately 4.54 billion years ago.

SEPTEMBER

The Moon

Earth's Moon forms early in Solar System history, probably following a giant impact involving the young Earth.

October

A living planet
~OCT 02

Early life

Earth's geological record preserves evidence for life billions of years ago. Microbial ecosystems dominate Earth's early biosphere.

OCTOBER

Oxygen transforms Earth

Photosynthetic organisms contribute to the accumulation of oxygen, profoundly changing Earth's atmosphere and oceans.

November

Complexity
NOVEMBER

Eukaryotic life

Complex cells containing internal structures evolve and diversify.

LATE NOVEMBER

Multicellular organisms

Life becomes increasingly complex, eventually leading toward the diverse multicellular ecosystems of the later Precambrian.

December

The final month
DEC 17

Cambrian diversification

Complex marine ecosystems diversify dramatically. The fossil record becomes rich in many forms of animal life.

DEC 18–20

Life moves onto land

Plants and animals progressively establish terrestrial ecosystems.

DEC 24

Dinosaurs

Dinosaurs appear during the Triassic and diversify across the Mesozoic.

DEC 26–29

Mammals diversify

Mammals evolve and diversify through the Mesozoic and Cenozoic.

DEC 30 · ~22:57

Non-avian dinosaurs disappear

Around 66 million years ago, the Cretaceous–Paleogene mass extinction eliminates non-avian dinosaurs along with many other groups.

DEC 31

Primates

Primates and other mammalian groups diversify within the Cenozoic world.

DEC 31 · 23:59:59

Homo sapiens

Our species appears extremely close to midnight on the Cosmic Calendar. Around 300,000 years of human evolutionary history occupies only a tiny fraction of the final day.

FINAL MINUTES

Agriculture

Agriculture, permanent settlements and increasingly complex societies appear only moments before midnight.

FINAL SECONDS

Writing

Recorded human history occupies only the final seconds of the Cosmic Calendar.

You are here.

The Universe has spent billions of years becoming increasingly structured. Stars. Galaxies. Elements. Planets. Life. Conscious observers. And now — this moment.

SEPTEMBER

Our star appears late.

On the Cosmic Calendar, the Sun and Solar System arrive during the final third of the year. Earth is not an ancient feature of the Universe. It is a relatively recent structure built from material produced by earlier generations of stars.

DECEMBER 31

The final day.

23:59:59

Everything humans have written, built, discovered and remembered belongs to the final moments of the Cosmic Calendar.

YOU

A cosmic event.

You are made of atoms forged through cosmic history. Hydrogen from the early Universe. Heavier elements created inside stars. Matter gathered into galaxies. A star formed. Planets formed. Chemistry became biology. Biology became increasingly complex. And eventually, matter became capable of asking where it came from.

The Cosmic Calendar is not saying that the Universe was moving toward you. It is showing how extraordinarily much had to happen before an observer like you could exist.

SCIENTIFIC SOURCES

The evidence.

The Cosmic Calendar is a visualization rather than a formal scientific timescale. The underlying cosmic history is based on observations and models of cosmology, stellar evolution, galaxy formation, planetary science and evolutionary history.
