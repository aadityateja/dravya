ADITYA

One human being · one trajectory · many excitations
↓ follow the trajectory

Aditya is one of the billions of so-called human beings living on planet Earth.

Origin
02
September
1999

Born in Mysuru — the heritage city of Karnataka, in the southern part of the Indian subcontinent of Asia.

From there began a trajectory that would pass through classrooms, failures, music, night shifts, cities, physics, stories, stars, darkness and eventually back toward light.

Once upon a time,
there was only one dream.

To become a filmmaker.

The trajectory

A life does not always move forward.

Childhood
The filmmaker

During his school years, Aditya was a studious child. Around the sixth grade, something else entered the picture. Films. Magic performances. Science fiction. Thrillers. Neo-noir. He began spending enormous amounts of time watching films and slowly became fascinated by the possibility of creating worlds of his own. The dream was simple: become a filmmaker.

2015
87.52%

He completed his 10th grade with a distinction, scoring 87.52 percent without private tuition. The result brought confidence. Perhaps too much of it.

2017
The first drift

He entered pre-university education. A much larger classroom. New people. New distractions. He began editing photographs using Adobe Photoshop and became known among his friends for his image manipulation and short videos made from random shoots. Somewhere along the way, studies began losing their place. So did a particular love story.

2017
30 / 100

The second PUC results arrived. Mathematics: 30 out of 100. Not enough to pass the examination by the ordinary requirement. His other subjects were considerably stronger, and he was declared an exemptary pass. While many of his friends moved toward engineering, Aditya entered B.Sc. Physics, Mathematics and Electronics at Yuvaraja College, Mysuru. He had little idea what he was doing.

2017 — 2018
Failed.

The first semester passed quickly. Then came the result. Physics. Failed. For the first time in his educational life, Aditya had failed an examination. The trajectory had changed.

2018
Another way to tell stories

The old filmmaking dream had never actually disappeared. If stories could be told through moving images, perhaps they could also be told through sound. He joined keyboard classes. He began learning music. Another excitation appeared.

2018
Again.

The third semester arrived. Along with the third-semester Physics examination came the re-examination for first-semester Physics. He failed both. Again. The marks continued to fall. His father became worried and asked him to start working part-time.

2018 — 2019
Two worlds

From around July–August 2018, he began working a part-time job. College in the morning. Bus home. Work from 5:30 PM to 10 PM. While his friends travelled to engineering colleges on their new motorcycles, he moved between two worlds. Student. Worker. Six months later, in February 2019, he quit.

2019
SWASTAIN

He formed a band with his school friend and college friends. They called it SWASTAIN. They jammed. They performed at college events. For a while, life was simply music. It was fun.

2019
The examination wall

The fifth semester brought a strange academic landscape. Physics. Electronics. Mathematics. Multiple papers. Previous-semester backlogs. He cleared his first-semester Physics paper, third-semester Physics, fifth-semester Physics Paper I, both Electronics papers and all three Mathematics papers. One Physics paper remained. Fifth-semester Physics Paper II. He failed it. There was still a make-up examination. Then the world stopped.

March 2020
The world paused.

COVID-19 lockdowns began. The band stopped performing. Colleges closed. The world went indoors. But life did not become easier.

April — June 2020
The editor

During lockdown, he was asked to work again. He joined a Kannada local television news channel as a video editor. He edited news footage from around Mysuru. Three months later, he left.

2020
B.Sc.

He completed his sixth-semester examinations in July. In September, he finally wrote the make-up examination for the remaining fifth-semester Physics paper. Then came another job. Another night. Another shift.

October 2020 — February 2021
The upside-down world

A night shift. 9 PM to 6 AM. Sleep during the day. Wake during the night. In December, the result arrived. Officially: B.Sc. graduate. But the job itself had begun to feel meaningless. In February 2021, he quit.

2021
Searching

April. A call-centre job. Then another lockdown. Work from home. Low pay. July. Quit again. The search continued.

2021 — 2022
Technical advisor

After two months of searching, he found another night-shift job. This time as a technical advisor for a web-hosting platform. It was different. He met good people. He learned. He stayed for around ten months. Then, in August 2022, he left again.

2022 — 2023
Ten months of silence

He stayed at home. For almost ten months. Without a clear direction. And in that silence came the questions that night shifts had kept away. What was he doing? What did he actually want? What was his life supposed to become?

April 2023
Bengaluru

Mysuru no longer felt like the place where the next chapter would happen. In April 2023, he moved to Bengaluru. Alone. A small single room in Jayanagar. Weekends in Mysuru. Another beginning.

June 2023 — June 2024
Adobe

He found another night-shift job. But this time, something strange happened. The client was Adobe. The same creative software company whose tools he had once used as a college student — sometimes through unofficial copies — was now part of his professional life. And now he had access to Adobe Creative Cloud. The old filmmaker was still there.

02:00 AM

One night, during a break, sitting on the rooftop canteen, sipping coffee,

he looked at the stars. and something happened.

It felt like a call from the stars.

A whisper.

We're always watching you. We know everything. We'll meet soon.

June 2024

Enough of living upside down.

He had saved much of his salary.

He quit the job.

He bought a laptop.

And then he made a decision that required more courage than anything he had done in years.

He would return to academia.

After almost four years away from formal education, he would study again.

Not because it was the obvious career choice. Not because the financial situation was stable. Not because anyone could guarantee where it would lead.

Because the storyteller was still alive.

For years,
the moon had been his sun
and the sun had been his moon.

He slept through the light.
He lived through the dark.

It was time to walk toward the light.

2024 — 2026

Study the story.

In September 2024, Aditya joined the Master's programme in Physics at Ramaiah University of Applied Sciences.

He had decided to return to the subject that had once been the source of his greatest academic failures.

Physics.

This time, he did not simply pass through it. He immersed himself in it.

Quantum mechanics. Nuclear physics. Particle physics. Mathematical frameworks. Scientific computation. The structure of matter. The fundamental questions.

He paid his own fees. It was difficult. Financially uncertain. Personally risky.

But the decision had already been made.

The old dream of storytelling had led him toward questions about existence. Those questions had led him toward physics. And physics had brought him back to the same question:

What is the story?

Now
Researcher.
Teacher.
Creator.

Aditya holds a Master's degree in Physics with a specialization in Nuclear and Particle Physics.

He currently works as a Research and Teaching Fellow at Naxxatra Sciences and Collaborative Research.

He communicates deep science through interactive mediums for school students, designs science activities and modules, studies theoretical physics, and explores ideas through code and creative technology.

But physics is not the only excitation.
