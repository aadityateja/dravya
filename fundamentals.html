WHERE MEASUREMENT BEGINS

Reality can be
measured.

Physics describes nature through measurable quantities. The International System of Units provides a common language for expressing those quantities.

Modern SI is built around fixed numerical values of fundamental constants. The units are therefore connected directly to properties of nature rather than to arbitrary physical artefacts.

DIMENSION

Numbers have
structure.

Dimensional analysis allows physics to check whether equations are physically meaningful.

[ E ] = [ M ] [ L ]² [ T ]⁻²
Energy has the dimensions of mass × length² ÷ time².

This means that the joule can be written as:

J = kg m² s⁻²

Derived units are therefore combinations of the underlying SI base dimensions.

THE IDEA
ℏ · c · G
Three constants.
A scale of reality.

From a small collection of physical constants, we can construct natural scales of length, time, mass and temperature.

The equations do not tell us that the Universe must end at the Planck scale. They tell us something more subtle:

these constants define a remarkable natural system of scales at which quantum theory, relativity and gravity become deeply connected.
