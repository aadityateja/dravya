DRAVYA / 001

Physics is not a finished story.

Every generation inherits a picture of reality. Then something refuses to fit. An orbit. A spectrum. A measurement. A particle. A field.

The disturbance forces us to construct a new framework. Classical mechanics. Electromagnetism. Relativity. Quantum mechanics. Quantum field theory.

This is the history of those disturbances.

c. 300 BCE
NATURAL PHILOSOPHY
ARISTOTLE · ARCHIMEDES · ANCIENT SCIENCE
OBSERVATION
Humans begin asking systematic questions about motion, matter, geometry, astronomy and the nature of the physical world.
FRAMEWORK
Nature is interpreted through geometry, observation, logic and philosophical principles.
DISTURBANCE → Can motion itself be described mathematically?
1543 → 1609
THE HELIOCENTRIC REVOLUTION
COPERNICUS · KEPLER · GALILEO
DISTURBANCE
The Earth is removed from the centre of the astronomical picture.
FRAMEWORK
Planetary motion can be described through mathematical laws rather than purely philosophical constructions.
T² ∝ a³
DISTURBANCE → What universal law could explain both falling bodies and planetary motion?
1687
CLASSICAL MECHANICS
ISAAC NEWTON
FRAMEWORK
Motion becomes a mathematical structure. The same gravitational framework describes phenomena on Earth and in the heavens.
F = ma

F = Gm₁m₂ / r²
ACHIEVEMENT
Mechanics, astronomy and terrestrial motion become parts of one mathematical framework.
DISTURBANCE → What happens when matter, heat and light enter the picture?
1800s
THERMODYNAMICS
CARNOT · CLAUSIUS · BOLTZMANN
OBSERVATION
Heat flows. Engines transform energy. Macroscopic systems exhibit irreversible behaviour.
FRAMEWORK
Energy, entropy and statistical behaviour become fundamental concepts.
ΔS ≥ 0
DISTURBANCE → What is matter doing underneath the macroscopic world?
1865
ELECTROMAGNETISM
FARADAY · MAXWELL
DISTURBANCE
Electricity and magnetism reveal themselves as interconnected aspects of a deeper field structure.
FRAMEWORK
Maxwell's equations unify electricity, magnetism and electromagnetic waves.
∇ · E = ρ / ε₀

∇ × E = −∂B/∂t
CONSEQUENCE → Light itself is an electromagnetic phenomenon.
1905
SPECIAL RELATIVITY
ALBERT EINSTEIN
DISTURBANCE
Classical ideas of absolute space and absolute time cannot survive the structure of electromagnetism.
FRAMEWORK
Space and time become aspects of a unified spacetime.
E = mc²
DISTURBANCE → What if gravity is not a force at all?
1915
GENERAL RELATIVITY
ALBERT EINSTEIN
FRAMEWORK
Gravity becomes geometry. Matter and energy influence the structure of spacetime, while spacetime determines motion.
Gμν + Λgμν = 8πGTμν
DISTURBANCE → The microscopic world does not obey classical intuition.
1900 → 1928
QUANTUM MECHANICS
PLANCK · EINSTEIN · BOHR · HEISENBERG · SCHRÖDINGER · DIRAC
OBSERVATION
Blackbody radiation, atomic spectra and microscopic phenomena refuse to fit classical physics.
FRAMEWORK
Physical quantities become quantized. The state of a system is described by a quantum state, with probabilities emerging from its mathematical structure.
E = hν

iℏ ∂ψ/∂t = Ĥψ
DISTURBANCE → Can quantum mechanics and special relativity describe the same world?
1911 → 1932
THE NUCLEAR WORLD
RUTHERFORD · CHADWICK · FERMI
DISCOVERY
The atom reveals a tiny dense nucleus. Later, the neutron joins the proton as a constituent of nuclear matter.
FRAMEWORK
Nuclear physics emerges as a new layer of physical reality.
DISTURBANCE → What are the particles inside matter really made of?
1920s → 1940s
QUANTUM FIELD THEORY
DIRAC · FEYNMAN · SCHWINGER · TOMONAGA
FRAMEWORK
Quantum mechanics meets special relativity. Fields become fundamental mathematical objects, and what we call particles can emerge as excitations of those fields.
ℒ = ℒ(field, ∂field)
NEW LANGUAGE → Reality can be described in terms of interacting fields.
1960s → 1970s
THE STANDARD MODEL
QUARKS · GAUGE FIELDS · HIGGS
FRAMEWORK
Electromagnetic, weak and strong interactions become components of a quantum field-theoretic description of elementary particles.
SU(3) × SU(2) × U(1)
STRUCTURE
Quarks. Leptons. Gauge bosons. Higgs field.
DISTURBANCE → The framework works extraordinarily well. But it is not the whole story.
2012
HIGGS BOSON
CERN · ATLAS · CMS
OBSERVATION
A new particle is observed at the Large Hadron Collider with properties consistent with the Higgs boson.
FRAMEWORK
The discovery provides experimental confirmation of a crucial part of the Standard Model.
AND YET → Dark matter remains. Dark energy remains. Gravity remains outside the Standard Model.
1998 →
NEUTRINO OSCILLATIONS
ATMOSPHERIC · SOLAR · ACCELERATOR EXPERIMENTS
DISTURBANCE
Neutrinos change flavour as they propagate. The phenomenon implies that neutrinos have mass.
CONSEQUENCE
The minimal Standard Model must be extended. A tiny property opens a much larger question.
QUESTION → What else is hiding beyond the framework?

Every framework
contains its own disturbance.
