ONE CALENDAR · 13.8 BILLION YEARS

THE IDEA

A calendar for
almost everything.

The Universe is approximately 13.8 billion years old. Earth formed about 4.54 billion years ago. Geological time then divides Earth's history into nested intervals — eons, eras, periods, epochs and ages.

Here, we compress the age of the Universe into one calendar year. Every event keeps its approximate physical age. The calendar simply gives our minds another way to see the scale.

THE TIMELINE

From the first moments
to the present.

These events are positioned according to their approximate age before present. Their positions on the cosmic calendar are calculated automatically.

~13.8 BILLION YEARS AGO

The Big Bang

The Universe begins expanding from an extremely hot, dense early state.

~13.8 BILLION YEARS AGO

Recombination

Hundreds of thousands of years after the beginning, atoms form and the Universe becomes transparent to light. The cosmic microwave background is released.

~13.6 BILLION YEARS AGO

First Stars

The first generations of stars ignite from primordial matter, beginning a new era of stellar evolution.

~13.5 BILLION YEARS AGO

Early Galaxies

Small early galaxies emerge and begin assembling into the structures that populate the Universe today.

~13.6 BILLION YEARS AGO

Milky Way

Our galaxy grows through the assembly and merging of smaller structures over cosmic time.

~4.6 BILLION YEARS AGO

The Solar System

The Sun and planets form from a rotating disk of gas and dust surrounding the young Sun.

~4.54 BILLION YEARS AGO

Earth

A rocky planet forms around the young Sun.

~3.7 BILLION YEARS AGO

Early Life

Geological evidence points toward very ancient microbial life on Earth.

~2.4 BILLION YEARS AGO

Great Oxidation Event

Oxygen produced by photosynthetic organisms begins accumulating significantly in Earth's atmosphere.

~1.8 BILLION YEARS AGO

Complex Cells

Eukaryotic life becomes an increasingly important part of Earth's biosphere.

~538.8 MILLION YEARS AGO

Cambrian

The Phanerozoic begins with the Cambrian and a major diversification of animal life.

~485.4 MILLION YEARS AGO

Ordovician

Marine ecosystems diversify dramatically.

~419.2 MILLION YEARS AGO

Devonian

Often called the Age of Fishes. Vertebrates continue expanding into new ecological environments.

~358.9 MILLION YEARS AGO

Carboniferous

Vast forests spread across parts of the planet, contributing to deposits that later become coal.

~298.9 MILLION YEARS AGO

Permian

Supercontinent Pangaea dominates Earth's geography. The period ends with the largest known mass extinction.

~251.9 MILLION YEARS AGO

Triassic

Ecosystems recover and dinosaurs appear during the early stages of the Mesozoic.

~201.4 MILLION YEARS AGO

Jurassic

Dinosaurs become major components of terrestrial ecosystems while the supercontinent begins breaking apart.

~145 MILLION YEARS AGO

Cretaceous

Flowering plants diversify and dinosaurs continue dominating many terrestrial ecosystems.

66 MILLION YEARS AGO

K–Pg Boundary

A major mass extinction marks the end of the Cretaceous and non-avian dinosaurs.

2.58 MILLION YEARS AGO

Quaternary

The current geological period begins. Ice-age cycles become a defining feature.

~300,000 YEARS AGO

Homo sapiens

Our species emerges in Africa.

~12,000 YEARS AGO

Agriculture

Human societies increasingly domesticate plants and animals and establish permanent settlements.

~5,500 YEARS AGO

Writing

Written systems emerge, creating durable records of human societies.

EARTH IN DETAIL

Zoom into
geological time.

Earth
~4.54 BILLION YEARS
Earth has existed for approximately 4.54 billion years. Its geological history contains enormous intervals that predate complex life, dinosaurs and humanity.
THE PHANEROZOIC

The visible
fossil record.

PALEOZOIC
~538.8 – 251.9 Ma
Marine diversification, the colonisation of land, forests, early vertebrates and the rise of complex terrestrial ecosystems.
MESOZOIC
~251.9 – 66 Ma
Triassic, Jurassic and Cretaceous worlds. Dinosaurs dominate many terrestrial ecosystems. Birds and flowering plants emerge and diversify.
CENOZOIC
66 Ma – present
Mammals diversify after the end-Cretaceous extinction. Primates evolve, climates shift and eventually humans become a geological force.
QUATERNARY
2.58 Ma – present
The current geological period, containing the Pleistocene and Holocene.
HOLOCENE
11.7 ka – present
The current geological epoch. It contains the Greenlandian, Northgrippian and Meghalayan ages.

On the cosmic calendar, one second represents roughly 438 years.

Agriculture occupies only the final few tens of seconds. Recorded history is only a handful of seconds. A human lifetime is measured in fractions of a second.
