01 — STORY

THE EYE OF THE BIRD

The forenoon breeze moved gently through the great trees of the Gurukul at Hastinapur.

Somewhere between the rustling leaves and distant birdsong, arrows were already flying.

The archery lesson had begun.

Under the watchful eye of Acharya Drona, the young Pandavas and Kauravas had been training with discipline and rigour.

Today's task was simple.

A wooden bird had been placed upon a branch.

They had to hit it.

Drona watched as the young students took their positions, learning the posture, the grip, the tension of the bow and the discipline required before an arrow could ever be released.

Slowly, the Sun climbed above the horizon.

Yudhishthira stood first.

Drona asked him, "What do you see?"

"I can see you, Acharya. I can see my brothers, the trees and the birds. I am waiting for the right moment to aim."

Drona simply nodded.

Then came Duryodhana.

"Acharya, I can see the whole forest."

Drona knew.

Nakula stepped forward.

"I can see the bird upon the branch."

Drona smiled.

Then came Bhima.

He looked toward the tree.

"I can see that apple over there."

For a moment, the Gurukul felt a little less serious.

Finally, Drona turned toward Arjuna.

Arjuna stood still.

No movement.

No distraction.

His eyes were fixed upon the target.

His posture was perfect.

Drona asked,

"What do you see?"

What came as a reply sent a chill through Drona.

His eyes remained fixed.

"I see nothing but the eye of the bird."

Drona smiled.

"Marvelous."

"Go for it!"

Like lightning, the arrow left the bow.

For a fraction of a second, everything seemed to freeze.

The arrow travelled straight toward the wooden bird.

And struck its eye.

Arjuna lowered his bow.

He looked toward his teacher and smiled like a child.

THE STORY ENDS.
But the question remains.
02 — INTERPRETATION

WHAT DOES IT MEAN?

Although we never know if this event really occurred somewhere in the past on this planet, what matters is the deeper meaning hidden inside the story.

This story speaks about focus.

About the ability to exclude everything that does not matter and identify exactly what does.

The bird represents a goal.

The eye represents the definition of that goal.

The forest, the breeze, the trees and everything surrounding it represent distraction.

Drona becomes the inner critic — the voice that asks the question:

What exactly are you looking at?

And Arjuna becomes you.

Success becomes almost inevitable when you know exactly what you want, define it clearly and aim with the exclusion of everything else.

04 — PHYSICS

THE PHYSICS

What appears to be a simple bow and arrow is actually a system of energy, deformation, motion, vibration and material behaviour.

01

Energy

Work done while drawing the bow is stored temporarily as elastic potential energy. When the string is released, that stored energy begins its transformation into motion.

02

Elasticity

The bow bends when force is applied. Its material and geometry determine how it stores and releases mechanical energy.

03

Motion

Once released, the arrow accelerates and carries kinetic energy through its trajectory.

04

Vibration

The release does not end the motion. The string and bow continue to oscillate, redistributing energy through the system.

05

Materials

Wood, fibres, composites and metals behave differently under stress and deformation. The material is part of the physics.

06

The Arrow

Mass, shape, aerodynamic stability and momentum all influence the flight of the arrow.
